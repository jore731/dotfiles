# Creator Space Innovation Methods

BASF Creator Space is a global co-creation program for outside-in innovation.
It covers the front-end of innovation: from exploration and ideation to
evaluation and lean validation.

## Source Handbooks

All process knowledge in this skill is extracted from the official Creator Space
handbook series (2016, v2.0), stored in:

```
notes/literature/creator-space-handbooks/
```

## The 8 Methods at a Glance

| Method | Purpose | Phase | Participants |
|--------|---------|-------|-------------|
| **Summit** | Explore topics with leading stakeholders; build networks; position BASF | Exploration | 30–200, external experts + BASF leaders |
| **Empathic Design** | Discover latent user needs through observation; generate first insights | Exploration | 2–6 researchers, 5–15 users |
| **Jamming** | Generate rough prototypes around a challenge with a diverse group | Ideation | 20–100+, cross-functional |
| **Customer Co-creation** | Co-create solutions with customers across multiple sessions | Ideation + Validation | 10–30, selected customers |
| **Idea Contest** | Collect ideas broadly via open online challenge | Ideation | Unlimited; internal or external |
| **Open Innovation Challenge** | Seek solutions from external innovators and startups | Ideation + Validation | External; open to all |
| **Idea Pitch** | Evaluate ideas in a live pitch event; award funding | Evaluation | 5–15 pitchers, 3–7 judges |
| **Business Solution Design** | Translate selected ideas into viable business concepts | Validation | 4–8 per team, cross-functional |

## When to Use Which Method

```
EXPLORATION NEED?
  → Understand a market, topic, or stakeholder landscape
    → Many experts, high visibility needed: SUMMIT
    → Understand users' latent needs in their environment: EMPATHIC DESIGN

IDEATION NEED?
  → Generate many ideas fast, tangible prototypes, fun format: JAMMING
  → Co-create with specific customers over time: CUSTOMER CO-CREATION
  → Broad idea collection, internal or external: IDEA CONTEST
  → Attract external innovators and startups: OPEN INNOVATION CHALLENGE

EVALUATION NEED?
  → Select the best ideas with funding as reward: IDEA PITCH

VALIDATION NEED?
  → Turn a selected idea into a business case: BUSINESS SOLUTION DESIGN
```

## Common 3-Phase Structure

Every Creator Space method follows the same 3-phase pattern:

```
1. PREPARE   → Before the event
2. EXECUTE   → During the event
3. MOVE FORWARD → After the event
```

## Method Workflows

### Summit

**Prepare (9 steps)**
1. Set the goal — what strategic question should be answered?
2. Define scope — which sub-topics?
3. Set up governance — steering committee, sponsorship
4. Build project team — organizing team roles
5. Select sub-topics — 3–5 focus areas per day
6. Identify participants — mix of internal and external, 30–200
7. Prepare input material — background papers, data briefs
8. Promote the event — pre-event communication
9. Define event program — 2-day structure with keynotes, discussions

**Execute (Day 1 + Day 2)**
- Day 1: Welcome, opening context setting; Share and Learn (presentations + discussion)
- Day 2: Inject outside perspectives (expert input); Discuss and prioritize; Present conclusions

**Move Forward (4 steps)**
1. Debrief the organizing team
2. Document the output — summary, quotes, action items
3. Evaluate the output — assess insights vs. original goal
4. Disseminate the output — press releases, internal reports, follow-up with participants

---

### Empathic Design

**Prepare (5 steps)**
1. Define the field research topic — what user challenge to explore?
2. Do your homework — secondary research on the context
3. Identify who to observe — 5–15 target users in their real environment
4. Set up exploration team — pairs of observers (one researcher + one domain expert)
5. Prepare for exploration days — consent forms, observation guides, camera

**Explore (3 steps)**
1. Observe — shadowing users in their natural setting; do NOT use surveys or interviews first
2. Document — photos, field notes, audio; capture verbatim quotes
3. Share exploration findings — team debrief, share raw observations

**Move Forward (2 steps)**
1. Analyze findings — affinity mapping, insight clustering, "How Might We" questions
2. Generate first ideas — ideation session on top insights

**Key principles**
- Observe first, ask questions later
- Capture the unexpected, not just what was expected
- Look for workarounds, frustrations, and unspoken behaviors
- Minimum 3–5 observation sessions for pattern recognition

### Jamming

**Prepare (6 steps)**
1. Set the goal — what problem/opportunity should the Jam address?
2. Set up governance — sponsor, decision-maker for follow-up
3. Build organizing team — Jam Master, team of facilitators (1 per 10 Jammers)
4. Craft the challenge — a compelling, open-ended challenge statement (the Jam prompt)
5. Identify Jammers — 20–100+; diverse, cross-functional, internal + external
6. Promote your Jam — pre-registration, teaser communication

**Jam (6 steps)**
1. Ignite — energizing opening, context presentation, rules
2. Group Jammers in teams — 5–8 per team, intentionally diverse
3. Let's Jam — ideate and brainstorm; quantity over quality
4. Build to think — make rough prototypes; no polished decks, only tangible artifacts
5. Share and refine — cross-team feedback loops
6. Showcase and close — all teams present to room; celebrate

**Move Forward (3 steps)**
1. Debriefing — organize Jam artifacts and learnings
2. Share the Jam's power — communicate results to wider audience
3. Take the best ideas forward — connect winners to next step (Idea Pitch, BSD)

**Key principles**
- Prototypes over presentations: build rough, tangible artifacts
- Time pressure is a feature, not a bug — strict timebox each phase
- Diversity is essential — mix functions, levels, inside/outside
- Fail fast: bad ideas are fine; move on quickly

### Customer Co-creation

**Prepare**
- Select 10–30 engaged customers as co-creation partners
- Define co-creation goal and session structure (multiple touchpoints over weeks/months)
- Prepare materials, NDAs if needed, collaboration platform

**Execute (multiple sessions)**
- Session 1: Inspiration and challenge framing with customers
- Sessions 2–N: Iterative co-design; rapid prototyping; feedback loops
- Final session: Concept validation and prioritization

**Move Forward**
- Synthesize co-created concepts into actionable briefs
- Communicate outcomes to customers (close the loop)
- Hand over to business development or R&D

---

### Idea Contest

**Prepare**
- Define the contest topic and eligibility (internal, external, or both)
- Set rules: submission format, deadline, evaluation criteria
- Set up online platform (e.g., Wazoku, Spigit, IdeaScale)
- Recruit and brief judges

**Execute**
- Launch campaign — communications, promotion, reminders
- Moderate submissions — respond to questions, boost engagement
- Evaluate submissions — judges score on defined criteria
- Select winners — deliberation and decision

**Move Forward**
- Announce winners publicly
- Communicate all results to participants
- Feed selected ideas into Idea Pitch or Business Solution Design

---

### Open Innovation Challenge

**Prepare**
- Define the challenge — a specific technical or business problem
- Scope eligibility — startups, academics, external innovators
- Launch via open innovation platform (e.g., NineSigma, InnoCentive, BASF's own)
- Offer prize or collaboration opportunity

**Execute**
- Run challenge period (4–12 weeks typical)
- Screen and evaluate submissions against defined criteria
- Shortlist top solutions for deeper evaluation

**Move Forward**
- Award winners
- Engage top solvers for proof-of-concept work
- Formalize collaboration agreements if applicable

---

### Idea Pitch

**Prepare (7 steps)**
1. Build organizing team — event manager, communications lead, facilitator
2. Set the rules — pitch duration (3–5 min), Q&A (5–10 min), evaluation criteria
3. Select judges — 3–7 judges; mix of technical, business, external
4. Launch call for participation — internal or external; open submission period
5. Communicate — promotion, reminders, FAQ
6. Select and announce finalists — review submissions, select 5–15 pitchers
7. Mentor pitchers — coaching sessions pre-event (structure, storytelling, slides)

**Execute (3 steps)**
1. Go on stage and pitch — each pitcher presents; strict time-keeping
2. Evaluate the ideas — judges score independently, then deliberate
3. Announce winners — live announcement; award funding or next step

**Move Forward (2 steps)**
1. Follow up with participants — feedback for all, especially non-winners
2. Communicate selected results — internal/external announcement of winners

**Evaluation criteria (typical)**
- Innovation: How new and creative is the idea?
- Feasibility: Can it be implemented?
- Impact: Business and/or sustainability value
- Team: Credibility and commitment of the pitcher

---

### Business Solution Design

**Purpose**: Transform a promising idea into a tested, investable business concept.

**Prepare**
- Select 2–4 ideas from Idea Pitch or Idea Contest as input
- Form cross-functional teams (4–8 per team): R&D, sales, marketing, operations
- Brief teams on methods: Business Model Canvas, Value Proposition Canvas, Lean Startup

**Execute (iterative sprints)**
- Sprint 1: Define the customer segment and value proposition
- Sprint 2: Map business model (BMC) — channels, revenue, cost, partners
- Sprint 3: Rapid validation — minimum viable product (MVP) experiments
- Sprint 4: Investment case — financials, roadmap, risks

**Move Forward**
- Present to investment committee
- Fund selected concepts for proof-of-concept development
- Archive others with learnings for future funnel

## Facilitation Tips (Across All Methods)

- **Challenge statement**: Make it specific enough to focus but open enough to inspire.
- **Diversity**: Mix functions, hierarchies, and perspectives — discomfort produces insight.
- **Time-boxing**: Use strict timers. Energy drops if phases drag.
- **Psychological safety**: State explicitly that there are no bad ideas.
- **Documentation**: Assign a dedicated documentarian; capture in real time.
- **Follow-through**: Every event needs a clear "what happens next" communicated at close.

## Linking Methods into a Pipeline

Methods can be chained:

```
Summit / Empathic Design
        ↓  (insights, challenge questions)
Jamming / Customer Co-creation / Idea Contest / Open Innovation Challenge
        ↓  (ideas, rough prototypes)
Idea Pitch
        ↓  (funded ideas)
Business Solution Design
        ↓  (investable business concepts)
```

## Quality Checklist Before Any Creator Space Event

- [ ] Goal is specific and agreed by sponsor
- [ ] Participant list is diverse (functions, seniority, internal + external)
- [ ] Challenge statement has been tested with 2–3 people outside the team
- [ ] Governance is clear: who decides on follow-up?
- [ ] "Move Forward" owner identified before the event starts
- [ ] Communication plan ready for before and after the event

### Inline Quick Reference

#### Challenge Statement Formula

Used in Jamming and Idea Contest. The statement must be:

```
HOW TO [action verb] [subject/group] [desired outcome] [context/constraint]?

Good examples:
  "How to enable smallholder farmers in East Africa to reduce post-harvest losses by 40% using affordable chemical solutions?"
  "How to make BASF's R&D onboarding process feel inspiring and memorable for new hires?"
  "How to design a sustainable packaging solution for agrochemicals that can be collected and re-used in rural markets?"

Bad examples (avoid):
  "How to sell more of Product X?" — too product/company-centric
  "How to solve all our packaging problems?" — too broad
  "How to implement our new digital strategy?" — too solution-prescriptive
```

**Checklist for a good challenge statement:**
- Framed from the user/beneficiary perspective, not BASF's perspective
- Open enough to allow diverse solutions (no implicit answer)
- Specific enough that Jammers can produce tangible responses in one day
- Tested informally with 2–3 people before launch

### Idea Pitch Preparation Timeline

```
T–16 weeks    Select judges
T–12 weeks    Launch call for participation (open 6–8 weeks)
T–6 to 8 wks  Close submissions; select finalists
T–4 weeks     Announce finalists; begin mentor coaching
T–1 week      Brief judges on evaluation criteria & scoring sheet
T–0           Event day: pitches, evaluation, award
T+1 week      Follow-up with all participants; communicate results
```

**Pitch event day flow:**
- 2–3 min: Host introduces each pitcher
- 5–7 min: Pitcher presents (strict time-keeping — horn/bell)
- 5–7 min: Judge Q&A
- 3 min: Transition
- After all pitches: Judges deliberate (15–30 min, not public)
- Award ceremony

### Idea Pitch Rules Categories

Every pitch must define rules in these five areas:

| Category | What to Define |
|----------|---------------|
| **Submission** | Topic, eligibility (internal/external/both), deadline, form fields |
| **Finalist selection** | Evaluation criteria, number of finalists invited, notification date |
| **Evaluation** | Number of judges, scoring rubric, decision process |
| **Award** | Funding amount, allowable spend, non-monetary rewards |
| **Next steps** | What happens post-award: PoC milestones, check-ins, reporting |

### Idea Pitch Submission Form Fields

Participants should submit:

1. **Short bio** (max 100 words) — or company profile for external participants
2. **One-page proposal summary** including:
   - The problem/opportunity addressed
   - The proposed solution
   - Why it is innovative (compared to existing approaches)
   - Estimated impact (business, sustainability, or both)
   - Team composition and relevant expertise
   - Resources requested (funding amount + intended use)
3. **Photo** (used on event page and printed programme)

### Idea Pitch Scoring Rubric (Typical)

| Criterion | Weight | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| **Innovation** | 25% | How new, original, and creative is the idea? |
| **Feasibility** | 25% | Can it realistically be implemented? Is the plan credible? |
| **Impact** | 25% | Business value and/or sustainability value if successful |
| **Team** | 15% | Credibility, commitment, and diversity of the pitcher/team |
| **Presentation** | 10% | Clarity, storytelling, and persuasiveness of the pitch |

> Tip: Share the rubric with pitchers in advance — it helps them prepare better pitches.

### Empathic Design Observation Guide

**Before the session:**
- Print observation guide (space for notes, sketches, verbatim quotes)
- Prepare camera, audio recorder (get consent first)
- Brief team: do NOT lead the interviewee; observe and ask open questions

**During observation:**
- Start by watching — resist the urge to explain, correct, or suggest
- Probe with: "Can you show me how you do that?" / "What were you thinking just then?" / "What would make this easier?"
- Capture verbatim: write quotes exactly as spoken, do not paraphrase
- Photograph context (objects, spaces, workarounds) with consent
- Note emotions and body language, not just words

**After each session (within 1 hour):**
- Team debrief: what surprised us? What contradicted our assumptions?
- Add interpretation notes — mark clearly: [OBSERVATION] vs. [INTERPRETATION]

**Observation prompt questions:**
- "Walk me through a typical day / a typical use of [product/situation]."
- "What is the hardest part of [activity]?"
- "Show me how you do [task]."
- "What do you wish was different about [situation]?"
- "Who else is involved when you [activity]?"

### Business Solution Design: Key Tools

#### 1. Buyer Experience Cycle (BEC)

Maps the customer journey across 6 stages:

```
Purchase → Delivery → Use → Supplements → Maintenance → Disposal
```

For each stage, assess across 6 utility levers:
- Customer Productivity
- Simplicity
- Convenience
- Risk reduction
- Fun & Image
- Environmental friendliness

Pain points and whitespace = insight candidates for new value propositions.

#### 2. Value Innovation Toolkit

Challenge the industry's taken-for-granted assumptions:

| Action | Question |
|--------|---------|
| **Eliminate** | Which factors should be removed that the industry takes for granted? |
| **Reduce** | Which factors should be reduced well below industry standard? |
| **Raise** | Which factors should be raised well above the industry standard? |
| **Create** | Which factors should be created that the industry has never offered? |

#### 3. Unit of Sales & Pricing Model Options

When designing the profit formula, consider:

| Model | Description | When to use |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Per unit | Traditional product sale | Commodity or transactional |
| Subscription | Recurring fee for access | Ongoing service or data |
| Outcome-based | Pay per result achieved | High-value, measurable outcome |
| Platform / marketplace | Revenue from transactions on platform | Multi-sided markets |
| Freemium | Free base, paid premium | Volume + upsell model |

#### 4. BSD Lean Validation Experiments

For each critical business model assumption, design a minimal experiment:

```
ASSUMPTION: [What we believe to be true]
EXPERIMENT: [Cheapest way to test it]
MEASUREMENT: [What data we will collect]
DECISION RULE: [What result would confirm / invalidate the assumption]
TIMELINE: [How long the experiment runs]
```

### Idea Contest: Community Engagement Tactics

The contest has three phases of community activity:

| Phase | Duration | Community Action | Organizer Action |
|-------|----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Submit | 4–6 weeks | Submit ideas | Respond to questions; boost engagement |
| Vote & Comment | 2–3 weeks | Rate, comment on, improve others' ideas | Monitor quality; remove spam |
| Evaluate | 1–2 weeks | (jury only) | Expert panel scores top-rated ideas |

**Community engagement boosters:**
- Respond to every idea within 48 hours (boosts submission rate)
- Feature "Idea of the Week" to showcase quality
- Share a mid-contest leaderboard to create momentum
- Include a "Wildcard" judge pick to reward originality over votes

### Open Innovation Challenge: Platform Selection Criteria

When selecting the web campaign platform:

| Criterion | What to check |
|-----------|--------------|
| Reach | Does the platform reach the target solver community? |
| IP protection | Who owns submitted IP? Check platform T&Cs carefully |
| Moderation tools | Can you filter, approve, and respond to submissions? |
| Analytics | Can you track views, registrations, submissions over time? |
| Cost | Setup fee + success fee (some platforms charge % of prize) |
| Integration | Can export submissions to internal evaluation tools? |

**Common platforms used:** NineSigma, InnoCentive, Wazoku, HeroX, BASF's own Inno-360
