# Idea Pitch — Reference Materials

## Preparation Timeline (16 Weeks)

```
T–16 weeks    Select judges
              → Approach senior candidates with a clear value proposition ("what's in it for them")
              → 3–5 judges; mix of internal decision-makers and external experts
              → Secure NDA if external judges will see confidential concepts

T–12 weeks    Launch call for participation
              → Publish submission form, rules, timeline, and evaluation criteria
              → Promote widely: all channels + Sponsor endorsement

T–12 to T–6   Submission window (6–8 weeks)
              → Monitor submissions; answer questions within 48 hrs
              → Do not extend deadline without strong reason

T–6 weeks     Close submissions; begin finalist selection
              → Organizing team reviews all submissions against eligibility criteria
              → Score against evaluation criteria

T–5 weeks     Select finalists (5–15 pitchers)
              → Deliberation meeting; document scoring rationale
              → Notify finalists and non-finalists with personalised messages

T–4 weeks     Begin mentor coaching
              → Assign 1 mentor per 2–3 pitchers
              → First coaching session: structure, story, slide review
              → Management approval needed for mentors' time commitment

T–2 weeks     Final coaching sessions
              → Dress rehearsal with timing enforcement
              → Judges briefed on topic context

T–1 week      Brief judges
              → Share scoring rubric + definition of each criterion
              → Share anonymised finalist summaries (not full pitches)
              → Confirm logistics: venue, schedule, deliberation process

T–0           Event day
T+1 week      Follow-up with all participants; communicate results
```

## Event Day Flow

| Slot | Duration | Activity |
|------|----------|---------|
| Pre-event | 30 min | Registration; brief pitchers on logistics; final tech check |
| Opening | 10 min | Host + Sponsor welcome; explain format and evaluation process |
| Per pitcher | 15–18 min | 5–7 min pitch → 5–7 min judge Q&A → 3 min transition |
| Break (if >5 pitchers) | 20 min | After pitcher 3–4 |
| Judge deliberation | 15–30 min | Not public; organizer provides scoring sheets |
| Award ceremony | 20 min | Announce winners; Sponsor presents award |
| Networking | 30+ min | Open mingling; pitchers available |

> Strict time-keeping is essential. Use a visible timer. Have the host ring a bell or use a card system. Judges lose confidence in the process if pitchers routinely overrun.

---

## Organizing Team Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|---------------|
| **Sponsor** | Provides funding mandate; is actively present; signs off award; acts as or supports judge selection |
| **Pitch Manager** | Owns end-to-end organisation; single point of contact for pitchers, judges, and mentors |
| **Mentors** | Coach finalists on content and delivery; 1 mentor per 2–3 pitchers; subject-matter + communication expertise |
| **Event Host** | Introduces pitchers; enforces time; moderates Q&A; professional communication skills required |
| **Support staff** | Venue, AV, catering, photography, live social media |
| **Legal** (if external) | IP review for external participants; NDA management |

## Rules Framework

Every Idea Pitch must define rules across these five categories before launch:

| Category | What to Define |
|----------|---------------|
| **Submission** | Topic, eligibility (internal/external/both), deadline, submission form fields |
| **Finalist selection** | Evaluation criteria used for shortlisting, number of finalists, notification date |
| **Evaluation** | Number of judges, scoring rubric, decision process (majority / consensus) |
| **Award** | Funding amount, allowable spend categories, non-monetary rewards, IP ownership of winning idea |
| **Next steps** | PoC milestones post-award, check-in frequency, reporting obligations |

> Publish all rules at launch. Transparency improves submission quality and prevents disputes.

---

## Submission Form Fields

1. **Short bio** (max 100 words) — or company profile for external participants
2. **One-page proposal summary:**
   - The problem or opportunity addressed
   - The proposed solution
   - Why it is innovative (compared to existing approaches)
   - Estimated impact (business value and/or sustainability)
   - Team composition and relevant expertise
   - Resources requested: funding amount and intended use
3. **Photo** for use on the event page and printed programme

> Provide the form as a template with field-level instructions — blank text boxes produce vague submissions.

## Scoring Rubric

Share this with both pitchers (before the event) and judges (at the T–1 week brief).

| Criterion | Weight | What judges assess |
|-----------|--------|--------------------|
| **Innovation** | 25% | How new, original, and creative is the idea compared to existing approaches? |
| **Feasibility** | 25% | Can it realistically be implemented? Is the plan credible given available resources? |
| **Impact** | 25% | Business value and/or sustainability value if the idea is successfully executed |
| **Team** | 15% | Credibility, commitment, and diversity of the pitcher or team |
| **Presentation** | 10% | Clarity, storytelling quality, and persuasiveness of the pitch |

**Scoring scale:** 1–5 per criterion (1 = does not meet; 3 = meets; 5 = exceeds expectations)  
**Total score:** Weighted sum, max 100 points

## Mentor Coaching Guide

Three coaching sessions are typical over 4 weeks:

### Session 1 — Structure (Week 1)
- Review submission form: is the problem clearly defined from user/customer perspective?
- Apply the "So what?" test to every claim
- Agree on pitch structure: Problem → Insight → Solution → Impact → Ask

### Session 2 — Story (Week 2)
- Open with a concrete story or scene — not a slide with text
- One central narrative thread — no detours
- Practice verbal delivery without slides (forces clarity)
- Time the full pitch with strict enforcement

### Session 3 — Rehearsal (Week 3–4)
- Full run-through with slides in event venue or equivalent
- Mock Q&A using likely judge questions
- Final timing check — cut anything over limit

**Likely judge questions to prepare for:**
- "Why hasn't anyone done this before?"
- "What's your biggest assumption that could be wrong?"
- "What does success look like in 12 months?"
- "What do you need from us beyond money?"
- "Who else is working on this?"
