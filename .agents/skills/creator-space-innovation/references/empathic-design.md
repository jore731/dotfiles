# Empathic Design — Reference Materials

## Observation Guide

### Before the Session

- Print observation guide (space for notes, sketches, verbatim quotes)
- Prepare camera and audio recorder — get written consent before recording
- Brief team: do NOT lead the interviewee; observe first, ask open questions later
- Review the research statement and the list of assumptions to test
- Identify the specific behaviours or contexts to prioritise observing

### During Observation

- **Start by watching.** Resist the urge to explain, correct, or suggest.
- Probe with non-leading questions:
  - "Can you show me how you do that?"
  - "What were you thinking just then?"
  - "What would make this easier?"
- Capture verbatim: write quotes exactly as spoken — do not paraphrase
- Photograph context (objects, spaces, workarounds, improvised tools) with consent
- Note emotions and body language — not just words and actions
- Look especially for **workarounds**: improvised solutions signal unmet needs

### After Each Session (within 1 hour)

- Team debrief: What surprised us? What contradicted our assumptions?
- Add interpretation notes — mark clearly: **[OBSERVATION]** vs. **[INTERPRETATION]**
- Flag top 3 most surprising findings while memory is fresh

## Observation Prompt Questions

Use these to deepen understanding without leading the participant:

| Situation | Prompt |
|-----------|--------|
| Starting the session | "Walk me through a typical day / a typical use of [product/situation]." |
| When stuck | "What is the hardest part of [activity]?" |
| To observe the real behaviour | "Show me how you do [task]." |
| To uncover latent needs | "What do you wish was different about [situation]?" |
| To understand context | "Who else is involved when you [activity]?" |
| To probe a workaround | "I noticed you did [X]. Can you tell me more about why?" |
| To go deeper | "What happens next? And then what?" |

> **Never ask:** "Would you like X?" / "Do you think Y would be useful?" — these lead to confirmation of your own assumptions.

## Team Composition

| Role | Profile | Count |
|------|---------|-------|
| Methodology expert | Experienced in observation methods; designs the exploration guide and leads team debrief | 1 |
| Cultural mediator / interpreter | Local knowledge, language bridge between team and participants | 1 |
| Technical experts | Domain knowledge relevant to the research topic | 2 |
| Documentarian (optional) | Photography, video, or audio capture | 1 |

> **Team size:** 4–5 people maximum. Larger teams make observed people uncomfortable and reduce the authenticity of behaviour. For complex systemic topics, run multiple small teams in parallel covering different target groups.

---

## Target Group Selection

| Guidance | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Include "extreme users" — people with unusual circumstances or constraints | Extremes reveal system failures invisible in average usage |
| Include non-users | Their rejection of the product reveals unaddressed barriers |
| For systemic topics: observe multiple actor types | e.g., both farmer and distributor in an agri-supply chain |
| Minimum 3–5 observation sessions before drawing conclusions | Single observations cannot distinguish pattern from anomaly |

## Desk Research Before Field Exploration

Before going into the field, build a briefing document covering:

1. **What is already known** about the topic? (literature, market reports, internal data)
2. **Who is working on it** inside and outside the organisation?
3. **What solutions exist** — and why have previous attempts succeeded or failed?
4. **List of assumptions** to test in the field
5. **Key quantitative data** (market size, user numbers, geographic context)

> Desk research prevents re-discovering known ground. It directs exploration toward the genuinely unknown.

## From Observations to Insights: Affinity Mapping

After all field sessions, use affinity mapping to convert raw observations into insights:

1. Write each observation on a separate sticky note (one idea per note)
2. Group related observations silently — no discussion yet
3. Name each cluster with an insight statement:  
   `[User] struggles to [X] because [underlying reason]`
4. Rank clusters by: frequency × surprise × strategic relevance
5. Convert top clusters into "How Might We" questions (→ see `references/challenge-statement.md`)
