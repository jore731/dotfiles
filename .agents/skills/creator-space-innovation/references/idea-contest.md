# Idea Contest — Reference Materials

## Community Lifecycle: 3-Phase Table

| Phase | Duration | Community Action | Organizer Action |
|-------|----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Submit** | 4–6 weeks | Submit ideas on the platform | Respond to every idea within 48 hrs; promote the contest internally |
| **Vote & Comment** | 2–3 weeks | Rate others' ideas; leave improvement comments | Monitor quality; remove spam; feature "Idea of the Week" |
| **Evaluate** | 1–2 weeks | Jury only | Expert panel scores top-rated ideas against criteria |

---

## Contest Architecture: Key Decisions

Before launching, the organizing team must decide:

| Decision | Options | Guidance |
|----------|---------|---------|
| **Scope** | Internal only / internal + external / external only | Internal contests first — easier to manage IP and community |
| **Topic specificity** | Narrow challenge vs. broad open call | Narrow = higher quality, fewer ideas. Broad = higher volume, lower quality. |
| **Platform** | Wazoku, Spigit, IdeaScale, Microsoft Viva Engage, custom | Choose a platform that allows idea *building* (commenting, combining), not just submission |
| **Prize** | Funding for PoC / recognition / non-monetary / combination | Funding is most motivating; recognition alone is rarely sufficient |
| **Jury vs. community vote** | Community-voted top ideas → expert jury selection | Both: community vote ensures broad engagement; jury ensures quality gate |
| **Duration** | Total 7–11 weeks | Too short = under-promoted; too long = community loses momentum |

## Submission Form Fields

Participants should complete a form with:

1. **Idea title** (max 10 words)
2. **Problem statement** — what problem does this solve, and for whom?
3. **Proposed solution** — describe the idea in plain language (max 200 words)
4. **Why is it new?** — how does it differ from existing approaches?
5. **Estimated impact** — business value and/or sustainability benefit
6. **What I need to implement it** — resources, expertise, funding estimate
7. **Team** — solo or team; names and functions

> Provide a template with explicit field instructions. A blank text box produces vague submissions.

## Evaluation Criteria (Typical)

Used by the expert jury to score shortlisted ideas after community voting:

| Criterion | Weight | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| **Innovation** | 30% | How new and original is the idea compared to existing approaches? |
| **Feasibility** | 25% | Can it realistically be implemented given available resources? |
| **Impact** | 25% | Business value and/or sustainability impact if implemented |
| **Scalability** | 20% | Can the idea grow beyond a pilot? |

> Publish evaluation criteria at launch — it improves submission quality significantly.

## Community Engagement Boosters

| Tactic | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Respond to every idea within 48 hours | Highest single driver of additional submissions |
| Feature "Idea of the Week" | Signals what good looks like; models quality |
| Publish a mid-contest leaderboard | Creates momentum and competitive energy |
| "Wildcard" judge pick | Rewards originality independent of vote count; prevents popularity bias |
| Host a live Q&A during the Submit phase | Clarifies rules; surfaces hidden barriers to participation |
| Personal outreach to high-potential non-submitters | Directly inviting specific people yields 3–5× higher conversion |

## Communication Plan Template

| Timing | Message | Channel |
|--------|---------|---------|
| T–2 weeks | Teaser: "Something is coming…" | Email, intranet, Teams |
| T–0 Launch | Full announcement: topic, rules, prize, how to submit | All channels + Sponsor endorsement |
| T+1 week | Reminder + early highlights ("Look what people are already submitting") | Email |
| T+3 weeks (midpoint) | Leaderboard update; "Idea of the Week" | Email + intranet |
| T+5 weeks | Final week countdown | Email + Teams |
| Voting phase | "Now rate your favourites" — transition announcement | All channels |
| Winners | Announce winners with name, idea summary, next steps | All channels + press if applicable |

## Post-Contest: What Happens Next

- [ ] Announce winners publicly (with their permission): idea title, team, what funding/prize was awarded
- [ ] Communicate to all participants: thank you + what happened to non-winning ideas
- [ ] Connect winning ideas to the next step: Idea Pitch selection, Business Solution Design, or direct PoC funding
- [ ] Archive non-winning but interesting ideas with tags so they can be retrieved in future contests
- [ ] Run an organizer retrospective: participation rate, idea quality, what to improve next time
