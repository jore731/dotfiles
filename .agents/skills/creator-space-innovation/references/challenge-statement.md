# Challenge Statement (How Might We) — Reference

Used as the primary framing tool in **Jamming**, **Idea Contest**, and **Open Innovation Challenge**. Also used to convert Empathic Design insights into actionable prompts.

## Formula

```
HOW MIGHT WE [action verb] [who/group] to [desired outcome] [context/constraint]?
```

## Calibration Examples

**Good examples:**
- "How might we enable smallholder farmers in East Africa to reduce post-harvest losses by 40% using affordable chemical solutions?"
- "How might we make BASF's R&D onboarding experience feel inspiring and memorable for new hires?"
- "How might we design a sustainable packaging solution for agrochemicals that can be collected and re-used in rural markets?"

**Bad examples (avoid):**
- "How might we sell more of Product X?" — company-centric, not user-centric
- "How might we solve all our packaging problems?" — too broad to prototype
- "How might we implement our new digital strategy?" — prescribes a solution

## Calibration Rule of Thumb

| Too narrow | Right | Too broad |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| Implies the solution | Points toward the problem space | Cannot be prototyped in one session |
| "How might we redesign the cap of Product X?" | "How might we reduce spill-risk when farmers apply liquid herbicide in the field?" | "How might we improve agriculture?" |

## Checklist for a Good Statement

- [ ] Framed from the user or beneficiary perspective (not BASF's)
- [ ] Open enough to allow many different solution directions
- [ ] Specific enough that a Jam team can produce a tangible prototype in one day
- [ ] Does not contain the preferred solution as a hidden assumption
- [ ] Language is plain — no internal acronyms or jargon
- [ ] Tested informally with 2–3 people before finalising

## Why "How Might We" (Not "How To")

- "How" signals the problem is solvable
- "Might" creates permission to experiment without commitment to a specific answer
- "We" signals collective ownership — not assigned to one person
